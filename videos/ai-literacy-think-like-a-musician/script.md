## 1:42 · Move 2: Play It Twice

*On screen:* Two chats ask "When did our town's library first open?" and get 1962 in one and 1958 in the other. Stamp: **FACTS CHANGED = IT WAS GUESSING**. Try it: treat changed facts as a rumor until you check them.

> Move two. Play it twice.
>
> Musicians replay a tricky part to see if it holds up. Do the same with AI. Open a brand-new chat, and ask the exact same question again.
>
> If the answers match, that's a good sign. If a name, number, or date changes, the AI was guessing. Treat that part as a rumor until you check it.

## 2:03 · Move 3: Flip the Key

*On screen:* Major (bright) vs minor (moody) chords. Two chats: "Why is studying in the morning better?" / "…at night better?" Both answers begin "Great question!" Stamp: **IT AGREED BOTH TIMES**. Ask neutral: "What are the pros and cons of each?"

> Move three. Flip the key.
>
> Switch a song from a major key to a minor key, and the whole mood changes. Questions work the same way.
>
> Ask: why is studying in the morning better? Then, in a new chat, ask: why is studying at night better?
>
> If the AI happily agrees both times, it's following your lead, not the evidence. So ask neutral questions instead, like: what are the pros and cons of each?

## 2:28 · Move 4: Find the Sheet Music

*On screen:* A **FACT RECEIPT** gets ticked off: opened the source myself · checked it's trustworthy · found the exact line · clicked every link the AI gave me → **VERIFIED**. The rule: No receipt? Don't repeat it.

> Move four. Find the sheet music.
>
> Before you repeat a fact from an AI, find the receipt. Open a real source yourself, like a textbook or a trusted website, and find the exact line that backs it up.
>
> A link from an AI doesn't count until you've clicked it and seen that line with your own eyes. No receipt? Don't repeat it.

## 2:49 · Move 5: Play It By Ear

*On screen:* Sheet music: "Reading along isn't the same as knowing it." Then a 30-second timer: "Explain it out loud" to a friend · family · your dog. Then: "Stuck? That's what to learn next."

> Move five. Play it by ear.
>
> A musician who can only play while reading the sheet hasn't really learned the song yet.
>
> So after AI helps you with something, close the tab, and explain it out loud in 30 seconds, to a friend, a family member, or even your dog.
>
> Wherever you get stuck, that's the part you still need to learn. AI should train your brain, not replace it.
