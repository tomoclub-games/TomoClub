```
One student filled almost every square of the grid. Another left gaps.

That was a Chrome Music Lab session where students built their own songs in Song Maker. When the sparse song played back, our facilitator Varchas Koushik said: "I like the gap in between where it plays and gives something to anticipate as well."

Anticipating what comes next is what a chatbot does all day. It writes one small piece at a time and picks from the likely options. So we turned that class into a short video for students: five habits for using AI, borrowed from musicians.

1. Guess the next note. AI goes for the likely note. The surprising one is yours to choose.
2. Play it twice. Same question, brand-new chat. If a date or number changes, it was a guess.
3. Flip the key. Ask "why is X better?", then in a new chat "why is Y better?" If it agrees both times, it's following the question.
4. Find the sheet music. No source you've opened yourself, no repeating it.
5. Play it by ear. Close the tab and explain it out loud in 30 seconds.

If you teach grades 6-12, #3 makes a ten-minute warm-up. Half the room asks one version, half asks the other, and you read the answers back to back.

The narrator in the video is an AI voice, and it tells students so at the end. For a video about questioning AI, that felt like the right way to finish.

Links to the full video and a longer write-up with a classroom version of each habit are in the comments.

#AILiteracy #K12Education #EdTech
```

## What we kept out on purpose

- **Results we haven't seen.** None of the copy claims what students said or what the Flip Challenge
  returned. The comments ask for results instead.
- **Common AI-writing habits.** No em dashes, no "in today's fast-paced world", and nothing like
  "unlock", "game-changer", "dive in", "it's not just X, it's Y", emoji bullets or hashtag walls.
- **Unverifiable quotes.** Every quote from the class is word for word from the recording.
