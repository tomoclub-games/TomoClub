What should a Grade 8 student let AI control?

TAICY 2026, our AI Innovation Challenge for Youth, is a wrap. Here's the moment that stayed with us.

In Round 1, students aged 8–15 had to spot a real problem, build a prototype with AI, and explain their thinking. One student noticed something every household deals with: it isn't always obvious which bin your trash should go in.

So they built a smart dustbin. A tablet camera spots the object, Gemini classifies it as wet, dry or electronic waste, and an ESP32 microcontroller swings a paper arrow toward the right bin.

But this is the line we keep coming back to:
"One of the things that I had to identify while making the app was what to let AI control and to not control."

AI did one job: recognising the waste. Everything else was deliberately kept out of its hands. That's AI literacy. Not just using the tool, but judging where it belongs.

How we judged Round 1:
→ Problem identification: 25%
→ Proposed solution: 25%
→ Working prototype & AI use: 30%
→ Pitch: 20%
Every entry also came with an AI Use Log: What did you ask? What did you change? What did AI get wrong?

Round 2 was live and team-based: multiplayer strategy games, no rulebook. Asked how they were moving so fast, one team said: "Because we remember where all the circuit pieces are, and exactly what we should do at which time." Then they cleared the same level with the lights off in 45 seconds.

Congratulations to our winners and to every young innovator who took on TAICY 2026. 🏆
