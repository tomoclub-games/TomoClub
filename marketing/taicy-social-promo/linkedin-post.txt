We give kids no rulebook. On purpose.

In TomoClub's live game-based sessions, students are dropped into strategy games with no instructions. To win, they have to talk, test ideas, split roles and adapt in real time. Every game then ends with a coach-led After-Action Review, where the play turns into skills.

We just ran TAICY 2026, our AI Innovation Challenge for Youth, for students aged 8–15:
→ Round 1 · Create: spot a real problem and build a working prototype with AI, logging what they asked, what they changed and what the AI got wrong.
→ Round 2 · Lead: random teams, live multiplayer strategy games, no rulebook.

A few moments from our sessions:
• A student built a quiz game with AI, for an English test.
• A team took a level that cost them 3 minutes and cleared it in about 1, once they started talking to each other.
• Asked what made the difference: "Because we were working together."
• The kids' verdict: "10 out of 10 out of 10."

AI literacy and collaboration aren't lectured here. They're practised, every week.
