## 1. LinkedIn: founder post (attach `carousel/amity-ai-literacy-carousel.pdf`)

> In August 2025, we asked the teachers at Amity School District in Oregon what worried them most about AI.
>
> The most common answer was cheating.
>
> Seven months later, an eighth grader in the same district wrote this on an exit ticket:
>
> "ChatGPT is a tool. I'm the one who still has to think."
>
> What happened in between started with a decision by Superintendent Jeff Clark that more districts should copy: train the teachers before you teach the students.
>
> 40 educators were trained in August. The student course started in October, taught by Amity's own teachers rather than outside specialists. 132 students in grades 8 and 9, 15 sessions each, with an ethics question in every lesson.
>
> Where things stood in March:
>
> - 87% average pass rate on eighth-grade exit tickets
> - The top-scoring session was an ethics debate, at 4.6 out of 5
> - 93% of educators surveyed said they understood AI better after training
>
> The part I keep coming back to: within their first few sessions, the ninth graders landed on the same worry their teachers had. One wrote, "Use AI for ideas not for answers." Nobody handed them that line.
>
> Superintendent Clark reviewed the full case study and cleared us to share it. The whole story, including the week the scores dipped, is in the carousel and on our Substack (link in the comments).
>
> Thank you to Darcy Hatch, Jill Whitehead and Mary Matocha for a year of great work.
>
> #AILiteracy #K12 #EdLeadership

Seven months later, an 8th grader there wrote: "ChatGPT is a tool. I'm the one who still has to think."

**2/** The superintendent trained the staff before any student lesson. 40 educators in August, from first-year teachers to a 31-year veteran, plus wood shop and ag teachers.

The student course started in October, taught by Amity's own teachers.

**3/** A baseline survey of 65 freshmen: 82% could tell helpful AI from harmful AI. Only 25% felt capable of creating anything with it.

High awareness, low agency. *(attach `carousel/04-agency-gap.png`)*

**4/** So every session was hands-on. Kids trained their own image classifiers, audited their own TikTok feeds, and took on an ethics question every week.

## Editing the visuals

Everything in `carousel/`, `images/` and `video/` is generated from the HTML in `src/`. To change a number or a line of text, edit the HTML and re-run:

```bash
cd marketing/amity-ai-literacy/src
NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node render.cjs all        # carousel PNGs + PDF, header, quote cards
FFMPEG=/path/to/ffmpeg NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node video.cjs   # reel (needs ffmpeg with libx264)
```
